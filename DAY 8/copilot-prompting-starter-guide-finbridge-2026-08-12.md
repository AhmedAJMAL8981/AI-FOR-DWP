# Copilot Prompting Starter Guide (FinBridge)

This guide is for everyday office work at FinBridge.
No technical skills needed. Start with the examples, copy one, and edit the parts in brackets.

## The 5-part prompt formula (easy to remember)

Use **SCOPE**:

- **S**ituation: what you are working on
- **C**hange wanted: what outcome you need
- **O**utput format: email, bullet list, table, draft, etc.
- **P**oints to include: key facts, deadline, audience, tone
- **E**xclusions: what to avoid

Quick template:

"I’m working on [situation]. Please [change wanted]. Give me the result as [output format]. Include [points to include]. Avoid [exclusions]."

## Outlook: 3 ready-to-use prompts

### 1) Rewrite a client update email clearly

"I’m sending a project update to a client who is short on time. Rewrite this draft to be clear and professional in under 140 words. Keep the deadline (Friday 5 PM), the action owner (me), and the request for confirmation. Avoid jargon and long sentences. Here is my draft: [paste draft]."

### 2) Summarize a long email thread

"Summarize this email thread into: 1) decisions made, 2) open questions, 3) who is doing each action, and 4) next deadline. Use bullet points and keep names exactly as written. Keep it short so I can forward it to my manager."

### 3) Draft a polite follow-up

"Create a polite follow-up email to [name] about the missing approval for [topic]. Tone: friendly but firm. Include that we need approval by [date] to stay on schedule. Keep it under 120 words and end with a clear call to action."

## Word: 3 ready-to-use prompts

### 1) Turn notes into a meeting summary

"I have rough meeting notes below. Turn them into a clean meeting summary with headings: Background, Decisions, Risks, Actions, Next Steps. Keep actions in a table with Owner and Due Date. Keep the tone neutral and business-ready. Notes: [paste notes]."

### 2) Improve readability of a draft document

"Edit this draft for clarity and readability for non-technical readers. Keep all facts the same, shorten long sentences, and replace complex wording with plain English. Then provide a short list of the top 5 changes you made. Draft: [paste text]."

### 3) Draft a first version of a policy update note

"Create a first draft of an internal policy update note for FinBridge staff about [topic]. Audience: all office staff. Tone: practical and friendly. Include: what changed, why it changed, what staff must do, and where to get help. Keep it to one page."

## Excel: 3 ready-to-use prompts

### 1) Explain what this sheet is saying

"Look at this worksheet and explain in plain English what is going up, what is going down, and what changed most by month. Then list the top 3 unusual changes and possible reasons. Keep it short enough for an email update."

### 2) Build formulas from plain language

"Please help me add formulas to this table. I need: 1) profit margin %, 2) growth % compared with last year, and 3) a simple status where negative growth shows 'At Risk'. Show exactly what to type in each formula cell and where to put it."

### 3) Prepare data quality checks

"Help me check this worksheet for common mistakes. Include checks for blank cells, duplicate invoice numbers, unexpected negative amounts, and dates outside this quarter. Give me a simple checklist and the formulas for each check."

## Data safety habit (from Day 1)

Before you prompt, pause for 10 seconds and check the data.

This is a safety habit, not a warning to avoid Copilot. Use Copilot confidently for day-to-day work in approved company tools.

- Use Microsoft 365 Copilot for work content in approved company systems.
- Do not paste confidential client data into external AI tools.
- If you are unsure whether data is confidential, pause and check with your manager or IT contact first.
- Remove personal identifiers when you can.

A safe default:

"Can you help me with structure and wording first? I will add sensitive details myself afterward."

## Quick starter tips

- Start with one clear task, not five tasks at once.
- Ask for a specific output format every time.
- Give the audience and tone (for example: client-facing, internal, executive).
- If the result is not right, ask for one focused revision.

Example revision prompt:

"Good start. Keep the same content, but make it 30% shorter and more direct for a senior manager."

## One-minute workflow

1. Pick one example prompt from this guide.
2. Replace bracketed parts with your real context.
3. Check data safety before sending.
4. Review the output and ask one revision.
5. Send or save the final version.

You do not need perfect prompts. You need clear prompts.
Short, specific prompts usually get better results.
